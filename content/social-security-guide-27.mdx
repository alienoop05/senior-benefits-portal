## What Changes Are Coming to Social Security in 2026?

Social Security undergoes periodic updates, reflecting economic shifts and policy decisions. In 2026, beneficiaries can expect adjustments in the cost-of-living and changes in full retirement age (FRA). The FRA is gradually increasing, affecting the age at which you can claim full benefits without reduction. This shift extends to those born between 1955 and 1960, gradually pushing the FRA from 66 to 67.

Cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs) are also expected to be a focal point. These adjustments, designed to counteract inflation, directly impact benefits. While the exact COLA for 2026 isn't determined until late 2025, understanding this mechanism helps in planning your financial future.

## How Are Social Security Benefits Calculated?

Social Security benefits are calculated based on your average indexed monthly earnings (AIME) during your highest-earning 35 years. The Social Security Administration (SSA) applies a formula to this average, resulting in your primary insurance amount (PIA). It's crucial to have a clear record of your earnings to ensure accurate benefit calculations. 

For those nearing retirement, reviewing your Social Security statement annually can prevent errors and ensure all earnings are correctly credited. Remember, delaying benefits past your FRA can increase your monthly benefit, up to age 70.

## Optimizing the Timing of Social Security Claims

One of the most significant decisions involves when to start claiming benefits. Claiming early (as early as age 62) results in reduced payments, while delaying up to age 70 can increase your benefits by approximately 8% per year. The decision should consider factors like your health, financial needs, and whether you plan to continue working.

Retirees often face the dilemma: should they start benefits early to have more money now, or delay to increase future payments? For some, especially those with health concerns or financial necessity, early claiming is reasonable. However, for others, particularly those with a longer life expectancy or other income sources, waiting can be financially advantageous.

## Understanding Spousal and Survivor Benefits

Social Security isn't just about individual benefits; it also includes provisions for spouses and survivors. A lower-earning spouse can claim benefits based on their partner's work record, potentially increasing their monthly income. This decision is complex, as claiming spousal benefits affects the primary worker's benefits.

Survivor benefits are another vital component. If a spouse passes away, the survivor may qualify for benefits based on the deceased's work record. Understanding these options can significantly impact planning, especially in dual-income households.

## The Impact of Medicare and Taxes on Social Security

Medicare and taxes are intertwined with Social Security. At age 65, most Americans become eligible for Medicare, and decisions regarding Medicare enrollment can affect Social Security benefits. For instance, Medicare Part B premiums can be deducted from your Social Security payments, influencing your net benefit.

Additionally, Social Security benefits may be taxable depending on your combined income. Understanding this tax liability helps avoid surprises at tax time and allows for better financial planning.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the full retirement age in 2026?**

The full retirement age (FRA) for those born in 1960 or later is 67. This is the age at which you can claim full Social Security benefits without reduction.

**How does the cost-of-living adjustment affect my benefits?**

Cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs) increase Social Security benefits to keep pace with inflation. The exact COLA for 2026 will be announced in late 2025, influencing the purchasing power of your benefits.

**Can I change my decision after claiming Social Security?**

If you claim benefits early but regret the decision, you can withdraw your application within 12 months. You'll need to repay any benefits received, and this option is only available once.

**How does working after retirement affect Social Security?**

If you work while receiving Social Security benefits before reaching your full retirement age, your benefits may be temporarily reduced based on your earnings. However, once you reach FRA, your benefits are recalculated to credit these months.

**Are Social Security benefits taxable?**

Yes, Social Security benefits may be taxable depending on your combined income. Up to 85% of your benefits could be taxable if your income exceeds certain thresholds.
