## How Are Social Security Benefits Calculated?

Social Security benefits are determined by your earnings history, specifically your highest 35 years of income. The Social Security Administration (SSA) uses your Average Indexed Monthly Earnings (AIME) to calculate your Primary Insurance Amount (PIA), which dictates your monthly benefit. Understanding your earnings record is pivotal. Ensure accuracy by reviewing your annual Social Security statement, which you can access online through mySocialSecurity.

### Maximizing Your Benefits: When to Claim?

Deciding when to start receiving Social Security benefits is one of the most critical decisions in retirement planning. You can begin claiming benefits as early as age 62, but doing so will permanently reduce your monthly benefit. Full retirement age (FRA) varies depending on your birth year, typically between 66 and 67. Delaying benefits beyond your FRA increases your monthly benefit by approximately 8% per year until age 70.

#### Considerations for Couples

Couples have unique strategies to consider. For instance, one spouse might claim benefits early while the other delays, maximizing the higher earner’s benefit. This strategy can provide immediate income while ensuring a larger survivor benefit.

### Social Security for Divorced Individuals

If you were married for at least 10 years, you might be eligible for benefits based on your ex-spouse's earnings record. This option can be advantageous if your ex-spouse's earnings were significantly higher than yours. Importantly, claiming these benefits doesn't affect your ex-spouse's benefits or those of their current spouse.

### Survivor Benefits: Securing Your Future

Survivor benefits are available to widows, widowers, and dependents of deceased workers. If you're a surviving spouse, you can start receiving benefits as early as age 60, or 50 if you're disabled. The amount depends on the deceased's earnings and the age at which you claim. Coordinating your own retirement benefits with survivor benefits can maximize your total income.

## Working While Receiving Social Security

Many Americans choose to work during retirement, and understanding how this impacts your Social Security benefits is crucial. If you haven't reached FRA and earn above a certain threshold, your benefits will be temporarily reduced. However, these reductions are not permanent; once you reach FRA, your benefits will be recalculated to account for months when benefits were withheld.

### Earnings Limits and Tax Implications

For 2026, if you are under FRA, $1 will be deducted from your benefits for every $2 earned above the annual limit, which is subject to adjustment. In the year you reach FRA, the deduction changes to $1 for every $3 earned, until the month you reach FRA. Additionally, be aware that Social Security benefits may be taxable depending on your total income.

## Anticipated Changes in 2026 and Beyond

Social Security faces ongoing financial challenges, with potential legislative changes on the horizon. While specific adjustments for 2026 are not yet confirmed, proposals often include changes to payroll taxes, benefit formulas, and cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs). Staying informed about these developments can help you adapt your retirement strategy accordingly.

## Planning for Longevity: A Realistic Outlook

Life expectancy plays a significant role in retirement planning. Many people underestimate their longevity, risking outliving their savings. Social Security provides a steady income stream that can help mitigate this risk. Consider your health, family history, and financial needs when planning for a potentially long retirement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I access my Social Security statement?

You can view your Social Security statement by creating an account on the mySocialSecurity website. This statement provides estimates of your future benefits and allows you to verify your earnings history.

### Can I change my mind after starting Social Security benefits?

Yes, you can withdraw your application within 12 months of your first benefit payment, but you must repay all benefits received. Alternatively, you can suspend benefits upon reaching FRA to increase future payments.

### How does remarriage affect Social Security benefits for divorced individuals?

If you remarry, you generally cannot claim benefits based on your ex-spouse's record unless the subsequent marriage ends. However, you may be eligible for benefits based on your new spouse's record.

### Is it true that Social Security is going bankrupt?

While Social Security faces financial challenges, it is not expected to go bankrupt. Without changes, the SSA estimates it can pay around 75% of scheduled benefits after 2035. Legislative action is necessary to ensure full benefit payments.

### What happens to my benefits if I continue working past full retirement age?

If you work past FRA, there is no penalty, and your benefits may increase due to additional earnings. Working longer can also increase your monthly benefit if your recent earnings are among your highest.
