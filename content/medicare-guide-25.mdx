## What Are the Basics of Medicare?

Medicare is a federal health insurance program primarily for individuals aged 65 and older, though some younger individuals with disabilities may also qualify. It consists of several parts:

- **Part A** covers hospital insurance, including inpatient hospital stays, care in a skilled nursing facility, hospice care, and some home health care.
- **Part B** involves medical insurance, which covers certain doctors' services, outpatient care, medical supplies, and preventive services.
- **Part D** provides prescription drug coverage through private plans approved by Medicare.
- **Medicare Advantage (Part C)** is an "all-in-one" alternative to Original Medicare. These bundled plans include Part A, Part B, and usually Part D.

### How Has Medicare Changed for 2026?

With healthcare costs continually rising, recent legislation has introduced changes aimed at improving Medicare efficiency and affordability. Here are some of the expected updates:

- **Premium Adjustments**: While Medicare Part B premiums typically increase annually, legislation may alter how these adjustments are calculated.
- **Expanded Coverage Options**: New plans and benefits, particularly regarding telehealth services and preventive care, are set to be introduced.
- **Enrollment Period Modifications**: Changes to the enrollment and disenrollment periods might affect when and how beneficiaries can adjust their plans.

## How to Choose Between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage

Choosing between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage depends on several factors including healthcare needs, financial situation, and personal preferences. Here's a quick comparison to aid in decision-making:

- **Coverage**: Original Medicare offers flexibility in choosing healthcare providers but may require separate Part D for drug coverage. Medicare Advantage plans often have network restrictions but include additional benefits like vision, hearing, and dental.
- **Costs**: Medicare Advantage plans might offer lower premiums but can include higher out-of-pocket costs, whereas Original Medicare has a standardized cost-sharing structure.
- **Provider Access**: Those who prefer a wider choice of healthcare providers might lean towards Original Medicare. On the other hand, Medicare Advantage plans may offer coordinated care at a lower cost.

### What Are the Common Mistakes When Planning for Medicare?

When planning for Medicare, some common mistakes can lead to financial pitfalls or gaps in coverage:

- **Ignoring Plan Details**: Overlooking the specifics of what each plan covers can lead to unexpected costs or uncovered services.
- **Underestimating Future Healthcare Needs**: Not accounting for potential health changes can result in inadequate coverage or financial strain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I enroll in Medicare for the first time?**  
You can enroll in Medicare during your Initial Enrollment Period, which begins three months before you turn 65 and lasts for seven months. Enrollment can be done online at the Social Security website or by visiting a local Social Security office.

**What is the difference between Medigap and Medicare Advantage?**  
Medigap, or Medicare Supplement Insurance, is additional coverage that fills gaps in Original Medicare, such as deductibles and copayments. Medicare Advantage is an alternative to Original Medicare, offering bundled coverage with additional benefits.

**Can I change my Medicare plan after enrolling?**  
Yes, you can change your Medicare plan during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7. Changes take effect on January 1 of the following year.

**What should I consider when selecting a Medicare Part D plan?**  
Consider factors like the plan's formulary (list of covered drugs), monthly premiums, annual deductibles, and pharmacy network when selecting a Part D plan.

**Is Medicare automatically deducted from my Social Security benefits?**  
For most beneficiaries, Medicare Part B premiums are automatically deducted from their Social Security benefits. If not, you'll receive a bill for Part B premiums.
