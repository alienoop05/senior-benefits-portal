## What Changes Are Coming to Medicare in 2026?

### Medicare Parts A and B: What to Know

Medicare Part A covers hospital stays, while Part B covers outpatient services. In 2026, eligibility criteria remain stable, but there may be adjustments in premiums and deductibles. It's essential to regularly check official sources or consult a professional for the most current figures.

**Common Scenario**: Many retirees mistakenly assume Medicare covers all medical expenses. However, understanding which services require out-of-pocket payments or supplemental policies is vital to avoid unexpected costs.

### Medicare Part C (Medicare Advantage) Updates

Medicare Advantage plans are an alternative to Original Medicare, offering additional benefits like dental and vision care. In 2026, expect plan adjustments based on provider networks and regional availability. Researching local plan offerings is crucial, as benefits vary significantly.

**Tip**: Review your healthcare needs annually to ensure your chosen Medicare Advantage plan still meets your requirements.

### Medicare Part D: Prescription Drug Coverage

Part D plans cover prescription medications, with changes expected in formularies and cost structures. It's advisable to review your current medications against plan offerings each year to minimize out-of-pocket expenses.

**Practical Insight**: Many beneficiaries overlook the annual "donut hole" coverage gap. Understanding this phase can help you plan for potential spikes in medication costs.

## How to Choose the Right Supplemental Plan

### Understanding Medigap

Medigap policies supplement Original Medicare by covering costs like copayments and deductibles. As 2026 approaches, comparing Medigap options can help fill coverage gaps effectively. Note that plan availability and pricing vary by state.

**Advice for Caregivers**: If managing healthcare for a loved one, evaluate whether a Medigap policy can alleviate financial burdens.

### Alternatives to Medigap

Other supplemental options include employer-sponsored retiree plans or Medicaid for those eligible. Each alternative has distinct advantages and limitations, requiring careful consideration of personal circumstances.

**Real-Life Application**: Combining a high-deductible Medigap plan with a Health Savings Account (HSA) may provide tax advantages and financial flexibility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Medicare "donut hole"?

The "donut hole" refers to a coverage gap in Medicare Part D, where beneficiaries may pay higher out-of-pocket costs for prescriptions after reaching a certain threshold until catastrophic coverage kicks in.

### How do I avoid Medicare late enrollment penalties?

Enroll in Medicare during your Initial Enrollment Period, which starts three months before you turn 65 and ends three months after. If you have "creditable coverage," such as employer insurance, you may qualify for a Special Enrollment Period.

### Can I switch between Medicare Advantage and Original Medicare?

Yes, during the Annual Enrollment Period (October 15 - December 7), you can switch between Medicare Advantage and Original Medicare. Consider your healthcare needs and plan benefits before making changes.

### What does Medicare Part B cover?

Medicare Part B covers outpatient services, preventive care, and some home health services. It's important to check specific coverage details annually, as they can change.

### How can caregivers assist with Medicare decisions?

Caregivers can help by staying informed about Medicare plans, assisting with enrollment, and managing medical records. Understanding healthcare needs and plan details enables caregivers to make informed decisions for their loved ones.
