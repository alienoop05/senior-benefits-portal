## What Does Medicare Cover?

Medicare is divided into several parts, each offering different types of coverage. Here's a breakdown:

### Medicare Part A: Hospital Insurance

Part A covers inpatient hospital stays, care in a skilled nursing facility, hospice care, and some home health care. Most people do not pay a premium for Part A if they have paid Medicare taxes for a specific period during their working years.

### Medicare Part B: Medical Insurance

Part B covers certain doctors' services, outpatient care, medical supplies, and preventive services. There is a monthly premium for Part B, which can vary based on your income.

### Medicare Part C: Medicare Advantage

Medicare Advantage Plans are an alternative to Original Medicare (Parts A and B) offered by private companies. These plans often include additional benefits such as vision, dental, and hearing coverage. They may also include Part D coverage for prescription drugs.

### Medicare Part D: Prescription Drug Coverage

Part D helps cover the cost of prescription drugs. It's offered by private insurance companies and can be added to Original Medicare or included in a Medicare Advantage Plan.

## Who is Eligible for Medicare?

To qualify for Medicare, you must be a U.S. citizen or legal permanent resident for at least five consecutive years and meet one of the following criteria:

- Age 65 or older.
- Under 65 and receiving Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) for a specified period.
- Diagnosed with End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) or Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS).

## How to Enroll in Medicare

### Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)

Your Initial Enrollment Period is a seven-month window that starts three months before you turn 65, includes your birth month, and extends three months afterward. Enrolling during this period can help avoid late enrollment penalties.

### General Enrollment Period (GEP)

If you miss your IEP, you can enroll during the General Enrollment Period from January 1 to March 31 each year. Coverage begins on July 1, but you may face late enrollment penalties.

### Special Enrollment Period (SEP)

Certain life events, such as losing employer coverage, may qualify you for a Special Enrollment Period. This allows you to enroll in Medicare without penalties outside of the standard enrollment periods.

## Exploring Supplemental Coverage

### Medigap Plans

Medigap is supplemental insurance that helps cover out-of-pocket costs not covered by Original Medicare, such as copayments, coinsurance, and deductibles. It's essential to compare plans carefully, as coverage and costs vary.

### Employer-Sponsored and Union Plans

Some retirees may have access to health coverage through former employers or unions. It's important to understand how these plans work with Medicare and if they offer additional benefits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between Medicare and Medicaid?**

Medicare is a federal program primarily for those aged 65 and older or with certain disabilities, while Medicaid is a state and federal program assisting low-income individuals and families with healthcare costs.

**Can I have both Medicare and employer health insurance?**

Yes, you can have both. Medicare may serve as your primary insurance, with your employer's plan as secondary, or vice versa, depending on the size of your employer.

**What happens if I miss my Initial Enrollment Period?**

If you miss your Initial Enrollment Period, you can enroll during the General Enrollment Period, but you may incur late enrollment penalties.

**Is it mandatory to have Medicare Part D?**

No, Part D is optional. However, if you choose not to enroll and lack other credible drug coverage, you may face a late enrollment penalty if you decide to join later.

**How can I avoid late enrollment penalties?**

Enroll in Medicare during your Initial Enrollment Period or a Special Enrollment Period if you qualify. Keeping credible coverage until you enroll can also help avoid penalties.
