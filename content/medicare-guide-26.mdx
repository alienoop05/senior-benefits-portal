## What is Medicare and How Does It Work?

Medicare is a federal health insurance program primarily for individuals aged 65 and older, though certain younger individuals with disabilities or specific conditions like End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) also qualify. Medicare is divided into parts, each covering different services. Here's a quick breakdown:

### Medicare Part A and Part B

- **Part A (Hospital Insurance):** Covers inpatient hospital stays, skilled nursing facilities, hospice care, and some home health care. Most people don't pay a monthly premium for Part A if they or their spouse paid Medicare taxes while working.
  
- **Part B (Medical Insurance):** Covers outpatient care, doctor visits, preventive services, and some home health services. Part B requires a monthly premium, which may vary based on income.

### Medicare Part D

- **Prescription Drug Coverage:** Part D is optional and provides prescription drug coverage through private insurance companies approved by Medicare. Each plan has its own list of covered drugs and costs.

### Medicare Advantage (Part C)

- **All-in-One Plans:** Offered by private companies, Medicare Advantage plans include Part A, Part B, and usually Part D. These plans may offer additional benefits like vision, dental, and wellness programs.

## How to Enroll in Medicare

### Initial Enrollment Period

Most people enroll in Medicare during their Initial Enrollment Period (IEP), which begins three months before the month you turn 65 and ends three months after. If you are receiving Social Security benefits, you'll be automatically enrolled in Part A and Part B.

### Special Enrollment Periods

You may qualify for a Special Enrollment Period (SEP) if you delayed Part B enrollment due to other health coverage, such as through an employer. This allows you to enroll without a late penalty once your other coverage ends.

## Understanding Medicare Costs in 2026

### Premiums and Deductibles

Medicare costs can change annually, and 2026 is likely to see adjustments. Premiums for Part B often increase slightly each year, and deductibles for hospital stays (Part A) may also rise. Keep in mind that higher-income beneficiaries pay more for Part B and Part D through income-related monthly adjustment amounts (IRMAA).

### Out-of-Pocket Costs

While Medicare covers many services, it doesn't pay for everything. You'll still face out-of-pocket costs like copayments, coinsurance, and deductibles. Considering supplemental insurance like Medigap can help alleviate some of these expenses.

## Choosing Between Medicare Advantage and Medigap

### Medicare Advantage Plans

These plans can offer extra benefits not covered by original Medicare, like gym memberships or hearing aids. However, they may require you to use a network of doctors and hospitals.

### Medigap (Supplemental Insurance)

Medigap policies, sold by private companies, help pay some of the healthcare costs that original Medicare doesn't cover. Unlike Medicare Advantage, Medigap doesn't typically include Part D, so you might need a separate drug plan.

## Why Annual Enrollment Matters

### Reviewing Your Plan

During the annual enrollment period (October 15 to December 7 each year), you can review and change your Medicare coverage. This period is an opportunity to switch between Medicare Advantage and original Medicare or adjust your Part D plan.

### Avoiding Late Enrollment Penalties

Failing to enroll in Medicare when first eligible can lead to penalties. For instance, if you delay Part B enrollment without qualifying for a SEP, you'll pay a higher premium for as long as you have Medicare.

## The Impact of Legislative Changes on Medicare

### Anticipated Policy Shifts

Changes in healthcare policy can affect Medicare coverage and costs. Keep informed about legislative changes that may alter how Medicare operates in 2026 and beyond.

### Preparing for the Future

Consult with a financial advisor to understand how anticipated changes could impact your healthcare expenses and retirement planning. Consider the potential need for long-term care insurance, as Medicare does not cover extended nursing home stays.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the eligibility requirements for Medicare?

Eligibility typically begins at age 65, though younger individuals with disabilities or specific conditions like ESRD may qualify. Ensure you understand your eligibility to avoid penalties.

### Can I have both Medicare Advantage and Medigap?

No, you cannot have both. Medicare Advantage plans often cover many of the same services as Medigap, so you must choose one based on your healthcare needs and budget.

### How can I reduce my Medicare costs?

Consider enrolling in a Medicare Advantage plan with lower out-of-pocket costs or choosing a Medigap plan to supplement original Medicare. Shop around during the annual enrollment period to find the best value.

### What happens if I miss the annual enrollment period?

If you miss the annual enrollment period, you may have to wait until the next period to make changes to your coverage. Exceptions are made for certain qualifying life events, which may grant a Special Enrollment Period.

### Is it possible to change my Medicare plan after the annual enrollment period?

Yes, you can make changes during the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (January 1 to March 31) if you have a Medicare Advantage plan. During this time, you can switch to another Medicare Advantage plan or revert to original Medicare.
