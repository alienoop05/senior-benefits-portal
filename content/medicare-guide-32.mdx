## What Are the Major Changes in Medicare for 2026?

The Medicare program is subject to periodic changes that impact coverage options, costs, and services. For 2026, several key updates have been introduced:

### Expanded Preventive Services

Medicare Guide 32 has introduced new preventive services, including screenings for mental health and chronic conditions. These services aim to promote early detection and improve overall health outcomes. Beneficiaries should review these additions to take full advantage of their benefits.

### Enhanced Telehealth Coverage

In response to the growing demand for remote healthcare, Medicare has expanded its telehealth benefits. This includes coverage for a wider range of services, allowing beneficiaries to access healthcare professionals from the comfort of their homes. Telehealth is particularly beneficial for individuals with mobility issues or those living in rural areas.

### Adjustments in Medicare Advantage Plans

Medicare Advantage plans, often referred to as Part C, will see adjustments in their offerings. These plans, provided by private insurers, often include additional benefits not covered by Original Medicare. It's important to review the changes in premiums, out-of-pocket costs, and network coverage to ensure your plan aligns with your healthcare needs.

## How to Compare Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage Plans

Choosing between Original Medicare and a Medicare Advantage plan is a crucial decision that can affect your healthcare and financial wellbeing.

### Original Medicare (Parts A & B)

Original Medicare includes Part A (hospital insurance) and Part B (medical insurance). It offers flexibility in choosing healthcare providers but may require additional supplemental insurance to cover out-of-pocket costs.

### Medicare Advantage (Part C)

Medicare Advantage plans are offered by private insurers and include all benefits of Parts A and B. Many plans also offer additional benefits like dental, vision, and prescription drug coverage. However, they may restrict you to a network of providers, which can impact your choice of doctors and hospitals.

### Key Considerations

- **Cost**: Evaluate premiums, deductibles, and copayments. Medicare Advantage often has lower premiums but may involve higher out-of-pocket costs.
- **Coverage**: Consider additional benefits such as dental, vision, and wellness programs.
- **Provider Network**: Determine if your preferred doctors and hospitals are covered under the plan.

## Important Enrollment Deadlines for 2026

Missing important Medicare deadlines can lead to penalties and gaps in coverage, so it's crucial to be aware of the enrollment periods.

### Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)

The IEP is a seven-month window that begins three months before you turn 65 and ends three months after. This is your first opportunity to enroll in Medicare.

### General Enrollment Period (GEP)

If you missed your IEP, the GEP runs from January 1 to March 31 each year, with coverage starting on July 1. Be aware that late enrollment penalties may apply.

### Open Enrollment Period (OEP)

From October 15 to December 7, you can make changes to your Medicare Advantage or Part D plans. This is an opportunity to adjust your coverage based on your evolving healthcare needs.

## Understanding Medicare Part D and Prescription Coverage

Prescription drug coverage is a critical component of Medicare, and understanding your options can prevent unexpected expenses.

### What Is Medicare Part D?

Medicare Part D provides prescription drug coverage and can be added to Original Medicare or included in a Medicare Advantage plan. It covers both generic and brand-name drugs, but formularies (lists of covered drugs) vary between plans.

### Choosing the Right Part D Plan

- **Formulary Check**: Ensure your medications are covered under the plan's formulary.
- **Pharmacy Network**: Verify that your preferred pharmacy is within the plan's network.
- **Cost Comparison**: Compare premiums, deductibles, and copayments to find a plan that fits your budget.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main difference between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage?

Original Medicare offers flexibility in choosing providers and is managed by the federal government, covering hospital and medical services. Medicare Advantage plans are managed by private insurers, often including additional benefits like dental and prescription coverage but may have network restrictions.

### How often can I change my Medicare plan?

You can change your Medicare Advantage or Part D plan during the Open Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7 each year. Additionally, there is a Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period from January 1 to March 31, where you can make changes to your Advantage plan.

### Are there any penalties for late enrollment in Medicare?

Yes, enrolling late in Medicare Part B or Part D can result in permanent penalties. These penalties are typically added to your monthly premium and can increase the cost of your coverage.

### Can I have both Medicare and employer health insurance?

Yes, you can have both, but how they work together depends on your employment status and the size of your employer. Generally, if you are still working and your employer has 20 or more employees, your employer insurance pays first, and Medicare pays second.

### How do I apply for Medicare if I am still working at 65?

If you are still working at 65 and have employer-sponsored health insurance, you may choose to delay enrolling in Medicare Part B to avoid paying premiums. However, ensure you understand the rules for delaying enrollment to avoid penalties later.
