## Understanding Medicare: The Basics

### What are the Different Parts of Medicare?

Medicare is divided into several parts, each covering different aspects of healthcare:

- **Part A (Hospital Insurance):** Covers inpatient hospital stays, care in a skilled nursing facility, hospice care, and some home health care.
- **Part B (Medical Insurance):** Covers certain doctors' services, outpatient care, medical supplies, and preventive services.
- **Part C (Medicare Advantage Plans):** An alternative to Original Medicare that includes Part A and Part B, and often Part D, provided by private companies.
- **Part D (Prescription Drug Coverage):** Helps cover the cost of prescription drugs.

### What Changes Can We Expect in 2026?

Medicare is subject to periodic updates, and 2026 may bring notable changes. While specifics can vary based on legislation, anticipate potential adjustments in premiums, deductibles, and coverage options. Stay informed about these changes by regularly reviewing updates from official Medicare sources.

### How Do I Enroll in Medicare?

Enrollment in Medicare typically begins three months before your 65th birthday and extends until three months after. Missing this window can lead to penalties or delayed coverage. Those already receiving Social Security benefits are automatically enrolled in Parts A and B. If you're not, you must sign up through the Social Security Administration.

## Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap

### What is Medicare Advantage?

Medicare Advantage (Part C) plans are offered by private insurers and often include additional benefits like vision, dental, and hearing, which aren't covered by Original Medicare. These plans provide an all-in-one alternative to Original Medicare and may include Part D coverage.

### What are Medigap Policies?

Medigap, or Medicare Supplement Insurance, helps pay for costs that Original Medicare doesn't cover, such as copayments, coinsurance, and deductibles. Medigap policies are standardized and sold by private companies. They do not cover prescription drugs, so you may need a separate Part D plan.

### Which Option is Right for You?

Choosing between Medicare Advantage and Medigap depends on your healthcare needs, budget, and preferred level of coverage. Medicare Advantage plans often have lower premiums but may require you to use network providers. Medigap offers more flexibility in choosing healthcare providers but usually has higher premiums.

## Navigating Coverage and Costs

### How Much Does Medicare Cost?

Medicare costs include premiums, deductibles, copayments, and coinsurance. Part A is usually premium-free if you or your spouse paid Medicare taxes while working. Part B has a standard monthly premium, which may be higher based on income. Costs for Parts C and D vary by plan and provider.

### How Does Medicare Work with Other Insurance?

If you have other insurance, such as through an employer or a retirement plan, Medicare coordinates benefits with these plans. The primary payer, whether Medicare or your other insurance, pays first, and the secondary pays the remaining costs, if any. Understanding how these plans interact can prevent unexpected expenses.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

### What are Common Medicare Enrollment Mistakes?

One common mistake is missing the Initial Enrollment Period, which can lead to lifelong penalties. Another is not understanding the difference between Medicare Advantage and Medigap, leading to inadequate coverage. Additionally, failing to review annual changes in Medicare plans can result in unexpected costs or loss of benefits.

### How Can I Avoid These Mistakes?

To avoid these mistakes, mark your calendar for enrollment dates, thoroughly research plan options, and review your plan annually. Consulting with a Medicare expert or financial planner can provide personalized guidance tailored to your unique situation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between Medicare Part A and Part B?

Medicare Part A covers inpatient hospital care, while Part B covers outpatient medical services. Together, they form the core of Original Medicare.

### Can I have both Medicare Advantage and Medigap?

No, you cannot have both. Medicare Advantage plans and Medigap policies serve different functions and cannot be combined. You must choose one based on your needs.

### When is the best time to enroll in Medigap?

The best time to enroll in Medigap is during your six-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period, which begins the month you turn 65 and are enrolled in Part B. During this period, you have guaranteed issue rights, meaning you can't be denied coverage.

### How do prescription drug plans work with Medicare?

Prescription drug plans, or Part D, are offered by private insurers and help cover the cost of medications. If you have a Medicare Advantage plan, it may include Part D coverage, but with Original Medicare, you need a separate Part D plan.

### What if I miss the Medicare enrollment deadline?

If you miss the Initial Enrollment Period, you can enroll during the General Enrollment Period from January 1 to March 31 each year, but you may face late enrollment penalties. Planning ahead can help avoid these extra costs.
